      Help

      Tap a heading to open it. Everything here is also safe to try – you can't break anything.

      Getting started in five steps
      1. Tune your instrument on the Tuner tab (or with your own tuner).
      2. On the Config tab, choose your instrument and the exercise – for example a G major scale, 2 octaves.
      3. Go to the Practice tab and press ▶ Play if you'd like to hear how it goes first.
      4. Press Start, wait until it says “Play now”, then play the exercise at your own speed.
      5. When you finish (or press Stop), your score appears under the music, with every note checked.

      The first time, your browser asks if the app may use the microphone – choose Allow. Nothing you play is recorded or sent anywhere.

      The four tabs
      • Practice – the music, the tuning bar, Start and Play, and your score.
      • Config – choose exercises, build your syllabus, and change how things look and how strictly you are marked.
      • Results – charts of how you are getting on, and a PDF to send to your teacher.
      • Tuner – tune your instrument, use the metronome, and set up the microphone.
      • Games – train your ear and your voice to recognise and find notes.

      On a very wide screen, Practice and Config are shown side by side.

      Choosing an exercise

      On the Config tab, under Exercise:

      • Instrument – violin, viola, cello, double bass, voice, or “other instrument”. The exercise starts on a sensible note for it.
      • Type – scale, arpeggio, chromatic scale, dominant 7th, diminished 7th, and (for some instruments) double stops, scales in thirds and thumb-position scales.
      • Tonality and Key – major or minor, and which key. For minor scales you can pick natural, harmonic or melodic.
      • Range – how many octaves (or to a 6th / 12th where that is used).
      • Start an octave higher – if you'd rather play it higher up.
      • Repeat the top note – play the top note twice before coming down.
      • When slurred – how many notes to a bow when you switch on Slurred on the Practice tab.

      If an exercise doesn't fit on your instrument, the app tells you – try fewer octaves or a different starting octave.

      Your syllabus (your list of exercises)

      The syllabus is the list of exercises you are working on, so you don't have to set them up every time.

      • Set up an exercise and press Add to syllabus. You can give it your own name.
      • Tap an exercise in the list to practise it. On the Practice tab, ◀ and ▶ move to the previous or next one.
      • Use ⚙ to change an exercise, ✎ to rename it, and the arrows to move it up or down the list.
      • Your teacher can send you a syllabus link – open it and the exercises are added to your list. Share syllabus makes a link like that for someone else (your scores are not included).
      • Export and Import save your list and its results as a file and load it again.

      Only runs of exercises in your syllabus are saved in Results.

      Practising
      • Rhythm buttons along the top: one note per beat, long notes at the ends (and on the key notes), two quavers, two quavers and a crotchet, two quavers and a rest, or four quavers on each note.
      • ⌒ Slurred switches slurs on and off (for the one-note-per-beat rhythms).
      • ▶ Play plays the exercise to you, with an arrow following the music.
      • Start: the app listens to the room for a moment (stay quiet), then says “Play now”. Take your time – it waits for you to begin.
      • While you play, the tuning bar on the left shows each note: in the middle and green is in tune, higher up is sharp, lower down is flat. The arrow above the music shows where you are, and the music scrolls along with you.
      • The run ends by itself when you reach the end, or press Stop.
      Your score explained
      • Total out of 100 is mostly about intonation (playing in tune – 75%) and partly about timing (keeping a steady pulse – 25%).
      • Errors take points off: extra notes that aren't in the exercise, and scratchy sounds between notes when you change bow. Small scratches only count at the stricter levels.
      • A note you didn't play counts as missing and scores nothing; a different note counts as wrong.
      • The chart shows each note: bars going up were sharp, bars going down were flat. Green is good, then amber, then red.
      • Cents measure how far out a note was: 100 cents is a semitone, so 10 cents is a tiny bit out and 50 cents is half way to the next note.
      • The notes in the music are coloured the same way after a run.
      • Unsteady means the note wobbled in pitch while you held it.

      Don't worry about one low score – everyone has off days. Look at which notes were out and practise those slowly.

      How strict should the marking be? (Precision)

      On the Config tab under Listening, the Precision slider goes from Beginner through Elementary, Intermediate and Advanced to Expert. At Beginner, notes that are a little out still get full marks; at Expert they must be very close.

      Start where you get a mix of green and amber, and move up as you improve. Your past results are kept for every level, so the charts are always right when you change it.

      Tick I'm using vibrato if you play with vibrato, so the wobble isn't counted against you.

      Tempo and the metronome
      • Without the metronome you play at your own speed – the app judges how steady you are.
      • Tick Metronome with one-bar count-in (Config, Listening) to play along with clicks. Set the speed with Tempo.
      • The same tempo is used by ▶ Play.
      • There's also a separate metronome on the Tuner tab for any practice, with beats in a bar and a Tap tempo button.
      What you see: name, key signature or full music
      • Show: just the name of the exercise, the key signature, or the full music.
      • High notes: Change clef (as in printed music) or stay in one clef with Ledger lines.
      • Show the next note's name helps if you're playing from memory.
      • The ◐ button at the top switches between light and dark colours.
      Results and sending them to your teacher
      • Pick an exercise at the top of the Results tab (it opens on the one you're practising), or Summary for everything.
      • The 3D picture shows your last 10 runs note by note: hills are sharp notes, valleys are flat. Drag it to turn it round.
      • The line charts show your scores over the last 2 weeks, the last 6 months, and all time.
      • Run in detail: choose any of your last 10 runs to see its full result again.
      • The Summary shows one line for each exercise in your syllabus.
      • Export PDF makes a report to send to your teacher. Put your name in Student name on the Config tab so it's on the report. More PDF options lets you choose which exercises and charts to include.
      Tuning your instrument
      • On the Tuner tab, press Start tuner and play an open string. The app finds the nearest string; tap a string to stick to it.
      • When the bar is in the middle and green for a moment, it says In tune ✓. It also tells you whether to tighten or loosen.
      • 🔊 plays a reference note to tune to by ear.
      • You can tune in equal temperament or with pure fifths (pure fourths on the double bass), and change the A (usually 440).
      Setting up the microphone (calibrating)

      Every phone, tablet and room sounds a little different. Calibrating helps the app hear your instrument clearly. Do it once on each device, and again if you move somewhere very different.

      1. Tune your instrument first.
      2. On the Tuner tab, press Calibrate microphone.
      3. Stay quiet for a few seconds, then bow each open string when asked – long, steady and at your normal volume.

      The app then tells you if anything needs attention, such as being too far from the microphone.

      Games: train your ear and your voice

      On the Games tab there are two games. Each level is a short round of questions. Score 70% or more to open the next level, and try for three stars: ★ 50%, ★★ 70%, ★★★ 90%. Your best score for each level is saved.

      🎧 Name the note – press ▶ Start to hear a note, then tap its name from the four choices. ▶ Play again plays it as often as you like.

      • Levels 1–2: the key is shown (level 1 also plays the key first), so you can work the note out from the key note.
      • Levels 3–4: two notes in the same key – choose the first, then the second.
      • Levels 5–6: no key at all – the hardest. Notes a semitone or a tone apart are offered, so listen carefully.
      • The notes are played in the sound of the instrument chosen on the Config tab.

      🎤 Sing the note – press 🎤 Start, then sing the note shown and hold it steadily. The bar shows if you're sharp or flat; when you've held it in tune for half a second it turns green. Any octave is fine.

      • Level 1: you hear the key and the note – just sing it back.
      • Levels 2–3: only the note's name is shown (as Do, Re, Mi… with the letter name underneath), with the key played or just shown.
      • Levels 4–6: two or three notes to sing in a row.
      • Level 7: no key – sing the named note from memory.
      • ▶ Hear the key plays the key again; Skip moves on. You have 10 seconds for each note.
      • The notes are chosen for your voice: measure your range on the Tuner tab first (choose Voice as the instrument).
      Singing
      • Choose Voice as the instrument.
      • First, measure your range on the Tuner tab: Measure my range asks you to sing your lowest and then your highest comfortable note, each held steadily for two seconds. The app works out your voice type and puts every exercise in the right place for your voice.
      • You sing to Do, Re, Mi… – shown under the music. Choose movable Do (the key note is Do) or fixed Do (C is always Do) under Sing with.
      • Tick Show letter names with the sol-fa to see “Sol · G4” on the tuning bar as you sing.
      • Singing is marked a little more gently than instruments.
      Other instruments

      Choose Other instrument, type its name, and pick its lowest and highest notes. If it's a transposing instrument (for example a B♭ clarinet, which sounds a tone lower than written), choose the Transposition so the music is written as you read it. The clef is chosen for you, or you can pick one.

      Using it as an app on your phone or computer
      • Android, and Chrome or Edge on a computer: tap ⬇ Install at the top, then Install now.
      • iPhone and iPad: in Safari, tap Share → Add to Home Screen → Add. The ⬇ button shows these steps.
      • Once installed it opens full-screen and works even without an internet connection.
      • The screen stays on while the app is listening or playing – during a scale, ▶ Play, the tuner and the metronome – and for 2 minutes afterwards, so your phone doesn't lock in the middle.
      • The microphone stays on while the app is open, so you're only asked once. You can turn this off in Config (Keep the microphone on…).
      • On iPhone in Safari, to stop being asked about the microphone: tap aA in the address bar → Website Settings → Microphone → Allow.
      Moving to a new phone or computer

      Your syllabus, results and settings are kept on each device. To move them:

      1. On the old device, Config → ⬇ Export everything. On a phone you can save the file to Files, AirDrop it or email it to yourself.
      2. On the new device, Config → ⬆ Import everything and choose that file.

      On iPhone, the installed app and Safari keep separate copies – use these same two steps to move between them. Calibrate the microphone again on the new device.

      Tips for the best results
      • Practise in a quiet room – no music, TV or talking in the background.
      • Put your phone or computer about 1 metre away, pointing towards your instrument.
      • Tune first, and calibrate the microphone once on each device.
      • Play each note clearly; a new bow on each note (or a clear change on slurs) helps the app hear where each note starts.
      • Phones find the lowest notes of a cello or double bass hardest to hear – moving a little closer helps.
      Something not working?
      • “No notes were heard” – check the microphone is allowed, play a little louder, or move closer.
      • “Microphone access was blocked” – allow the microphone for this site in your browser's settings, then try again.
      • Everything sounded too high or low – the app warns you if every note was out by the same amount. Check your instrument is tuned and the Tuning A is right.
      • No sound from ▶ Play on iPhone – turn the volume up; it plays even with the silent switch on.
      • It stopped listening after you switched apps – just press Start again.
      • The phone locks while you're playing – the app keeps the screen on by itself on recent phones (iPhone: iOS 18.4 or later for the Home Screen app, iOS 16.4 in Safari). On an older iPhone, set Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock to a longer time while you practise.
      • A result looks wrong – your teacher may ask you to switch on Test mode (Config, Fault-finding). It keeps a recording of each run that you can save and send, so the problem can be found and fixed.
      Your privacy

      Everything happens on your own device. Your playing is listened to while you practise but never recorded or sent anywhere (unless you switch on Test mode and choose to save a recording yourself). Your syllabus, scores and settings stay in your browser.

      Everything happens in your browser – no sound or data is sent anywhere. Pitches are measured in equal temperament against the A you set; notes tuned as pure fifths can read a few cents away from equal temperament.
